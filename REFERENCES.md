# Adirondak Loj scene references

## Exact register structure

[Pat Bradley / WAMC, August 9, 2019](https://www.wamc.org/north-country-news/2019-08-09/new-initiative-targets-hiker-education-to-relieve-adirondack-high-peaks-overcrowding) includes a photograph captioned “Adirondack Loj trailhead register.” It was inspected as a modeling reference only; it is not included in the website assets and no reuse license was identified.

The photographed register is a substantial dark brown timber shelter, with a pitched shingle roof, square posts, diagonal knee braces, a raised wooden platform and stone/concrete footings. The front fascia has yellow “REGISTER HERE” lettering. The rear information wall contains notices and a large pale topographic map. A notice panel extends to the right. The front-right post has two yellow signs about leashed dogs and winter traction equipment. Dense slender trees, exposed roots, dirt, and scattered stones surround the structure.

The implemented scene is an interpretation, not a measured or photogrammetric reconstruction. The 2019 photograph does not establish today's exact condition or site layout.

## Trail topology

- [Adirondack Mountain Club: Avalanche Pass Trail](https://adk.org/avalanche-pass-trail/) identifies the Van Hoevenberg Trail (61) at the end of Lot 1, following blue markers to Marcy Dam, then the Avalanche Pass Trail (68).
- [Lake Placid: Algonquin Peak](https://www.lakeplacid.com/hiking/algonquin-mountain) describes a shared initial approach toward Marcy Dam, with the Algonquin junction at approximately one mile.
- [ADK parking information](https://adk.org/tips-for-hiker-parking-adirondak-loj/) identifies the High Peaks Information Center and hiker parking context.

The scene map is a schematic: Loj → shared trail → branch to Algonquin; the other branch continues to Marcy Dam → Avalanche Pass → Avalanche Lake → Lake Colden. It is not a navigation map or a claim that the full route is walkable in this demo.
